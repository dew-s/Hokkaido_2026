D4Oct 18Sun7
🏡 Higashikawa · night 2 of 3
🥾 Asahidake — the Sugatami Pond loop, 1.7 km of flat boardwalk with steam vents and the peak mirrored in the water. This is the star walk of the trip. 🍁
08:00 depart (~45 min) · 09:00 ropeway up 🚡 · 09:20–11:00 Sugatami loop · 11:30 lunch at the base station · 14:00 down to Biei (~1 hr 15) · 15:30 Shikisai-no-Oka · 16:30 Shin'ei-no-Oka · 17:20 back
Sugatami Pond
姿見の池 — 1.7 km, ~1 hr, flat. The ropeway is the only one in Japan running above the treeline, 1,100 m → 1,600 m in 10 min
Tenninkyō
天人峡 — gorge ~30 min on, same valley road. Hagoromo Falls 羽衣の滝 is a top-100 Japanese waterfall ⚠️ trail access has had rockfall closures — verify first
Forest God
森の神様 — a katsura tree said to be 900 years old, in the Tenninkyō woods. Bear country, so don't wander
Shikisai-no-Oka
四季彩の丘 — 7 ha of banded flower beds on Panorama Road, free entry and parking from Oct 1, open 8:40–17:00. The rainbow carpet is a July thing; by your dates expect crimson kochia, thinned dahlia and cleome, ochre bands, and first snow on Tokachidake behind. Flowers hold until first frost — Biei's usually lands around Oct 10–13, so check shikisainooka.jp the week before ⚠️ walking into the beds to shoot is banned — paths only
Sunset Shin'ei-no-Oka 新栄の丘 16:43

Five minutes on from Shikisai. The Biei sunset hill — 360° from the crest, west side for the sun, east for Tokachidake. Free, ~30 car spaces. Gates close at sunset, so be parked by 16:15.

Get to Shikisai by 15:30 and look east first: the low western sun puts warm light on the Tokachidake range with the beds in front of you. Then hop over for the sun itself.

Hokusei-no-Oka (~28 min from base) is the shorter alternative if the day has run long. No sunrise today — an 08:00 start beats a 35-minute pre-dawn drive.

🗓️ It's a Sunday, in peak foliage season. Asahidake is the single most popular walk in Daisetsuzan — be at the ropeway for the 09:00 car or expect to queue. Going earlier is better if the first run allows.

D5Oct 19Mon7
🏡 Higashikawa · night 3 of 3
💧 Blue Pond at first light, up the volcano road for the Tokachidake strolls, then a soak at Ryounkaku ⭐ — the highest onsen inn in Hokkaido. ♨️
04:20 pre-dawn out ⏰ · 05:30 Senbō Pass · 05:48 sunrise · 06:15 leave · 06:50 Blue Pond · 08:30 Shirahige Falls · 09:45–11:15 Bogakudai · 11:45 Ryounkaku ♨️ · 14:00 descend · 15:30 back
Senbō Pass
千望峠 — Kamifurano, ~1 hr 10 from base. A quiet roadside deck with the whole Tokachidake range laid out in front of you, lit head-on at dawn 📷
Blue Pond
青い池 — gates open 7:00. Arrive first and you get mirror-calm water with nobody in frame. Not naturally occurring: it formed behind a mudflow dam in the 1990s, and the blue comes from aluminium in the groundwater
Shirahige Falls
白ひげの滝 — 30 m, ~3 km upstream. Spring water pours straight out of the cliff face. The Blue River bridge below is the vantage point 💦
Bogakudai
望岳台 — volcanic gravel slopes, 20–40 min strolls, wide open views back down the valley
Ryounkaku
凌雲閣 — Hokkaido's highest onsen inn, day-use available ⭐ must-soak
Sunrise Senbō Pass 千望峠 05:48 ★

The Tokachidake range front-lit across the Kamifurano basin. Then you drive on and reach Blue Pond at 06:50, ahead of the 7:00 gate — two subjects in one morning, and the pond to yourself.

Costs a 04:20 departure. If that's too much, skip Senbō and leave at 06:15 for the pond alone.

Sunset Shirahige Falls 白ひげの滝 16:41 ★

Lit from sunset to 21:00 all year round — your one shot at illuminated water on this trip. Worth doubling back for on the way down.

Blue Pond's own illumination starts Oct 22, three days after you pass through. Just missed it.

✅ A Monday, not a weekend — Blue Pond will be far kinder than it would have been two days earlier.

09:00 depart · 10:30 arrive, lunch · 11:00 check in · 13:00 ropeway 🚡 · 14:00 5th-station loop · 16:30 last descent · eve soak
Kurodake 5th stn
黒岳五合目 — 1,300 m, an easy ~30 min loop plus a viewing deck. Ropeway runs 8:00–16:30. Kurodake colours earliest of any peak in Japan, so expect snow-dust over bare rock rather than autumn reds ❄️
Sōunkyō gorge
層雲峡 — 24 km of columnar-jointed cliffs along the Ishikari River, walls up to 200 m. The name means "gorge in the clouds"
Sunrise Shin'ei-no-Oka, facing east 05:50 ★ optional

Your last Biei morning — the Daisetsu and Tokachidake ranges take first light across the hills. Costs a 05:00 alarm and a 35-minute drive.

With seven people, make it opt-in: photographers take one car, everyone else sleeps and meets at breakfast. Push departure to 09:30.

No light Gorge blocks the west 16:37

Sōunkyō sits deep inside the gorge — the walls block the western horizon completely.

🚡 The pair lift from the 5th to the 7th station is closed Oct 16 – Nov 30. You reach the 5th station only. The easy loop and the deck are both still open — that was always the actual walk.
Drive + trek
D7Oct 21Wed7
♨️ Sōunkyō Onsen · night 2 of 2
📷 Your densest photography day. A pass at dawn, then the two falls in the only good light they get. 🍁
04:45 depart ⏰ · 05:20 Mikuni Pass · 05:43 sunrise · 07:00 back · 07:30–09:30 Ginga & Ryūsei · 09:30 Sōbakudai · 12:00 lunch · 14:00 Ōbako · 15:00 Visitor Center · 16:00 long soak ♨️
The two falls
銀河の滝・流星の滝 — both on Japan's top-100 list, side by side. Ginga falls ~120 m in fine white threads (the "female fall"); Ryūsei drops ~90 m as one thick column (the "male fall"). Together they're the married couple falls 夫婦滝 💦 (a waterfall database lists Ginga at 104 m — the tourism association says 120 m)
Sōbakudai
双瀑台 — the only place you see both falls at once. 20–25 min up a zigzag slope, 650 m → 788 m. Steep and slippery after rain, so proper soles ⚠️ bears are sighted here — carry a bell, and check the Visitor Center board first
Mikuni Pass
三国峠 — 1,139 m, the highest national-road pass in Hokkaido, ~31 km / 40 min south on Route 273. A known sea-of-clouds spot; Matsumi Bridge arcs over unbroken fir and spruce. Observatory with toilets, café and free parking for 20–30 cars. No winter closure ⚠️ 1,139 m before dawn in late October means real ice risk
Ōbako
大函 — the biggest cliff walls in the gorge, ~8 km on. Parking, toilets and a shop. There's also a cycling road from Sōunkyō if anyone fancies it 🚲
Visitor Center
層雲峡ビジターセンター — bear reports, trail closures and today's foliage status. Worth 30 min
Sunrise Mikuni Pass 三国峠 05:43 ★★

At 1,139 m you are well above the gorge that blocks everything else here. A genuine chance of a cloud sea, with Matsumi Bridge arcing over an ocean of conifer.

Foliage at this altitude peaks late September, so you will be past it. Skip this and start at 07:30 if you would rather have the lie-in.

Morning light Ginga & Ryūsei Falls 07:30–09:30 ★

The falls are a morning subject. The gorge runs roughly east–west, so low morning sun travels along it and front-lights the cliff faces. By mid-afternoon the sun is behind them and photographers record the light as flatly backlit and poor.

Early also means empty. Bring an ND filter — 120 m of falling water wants a slow shutter.

No light Nothing at sunset 16:35

Cliffs over 100 m wall the western end of the gorge. Soak through sunset — you have been up since 04:45.

🚧 Kobako 小函 is dropped from this plan — its walking path has been closed for years due to rockfall, and the highway now tunnels straight past the old scenic core. Ōbako is the one that's still open.

♨️ Noboribetsu Onsen · 1 night
🌅 Alpenglow on Mt. Yōtei before breakfast, Lake Tōya at peak colour through the middle of the day, then sulphur and steam to finish. 🍁🌋
05:15 pre-dawn out ⏰ · 05:52 sunrise · 07:15 back for breakfast · 09:30 check out · 11:00 Lake Tōya · 12:30 Nakajima cruise 🚢 · 15:00 scout Orofure · 16:00 check in · 16:42 Jigokudani
Nakajima cruise
中島巡り遊覧船 — 30 min departures, 08:30–16:30. 50 min without landing, ~80 min if you step onto the island. Island landing runs only to Oct 31, so you just make it 🚢
Silo Observatory
サイロ展望台 — the classic high view over the caldera lake and its islands
Lakebed rails
湖底線路 — old track running down into the water on the south shore, with Mt. Yōtei behind it on a clear day. Odd and very photogenic 📷
Jigokudani
地獄谷 — "hell valley", an explosion crater venting steam and sulphur. Boardwalks throughout, 5 min on foot from the onsen street 🌋
Ōyunuma
大湯沼 — a sulphur pond at 130 °C on the floor. The natural foot bath 天然足湯 downstream is free and sits in the woods, ~20 min walk on
Sunrise Nakayama Pass 中山峠 05:52 ★

The sun comes up behind you, so it doesn't rise over anything — it lands on Mt. Yōtei instead. Alpenglow on a near-symmetrical cone, ~30 min from the ryokan.

Leaving at 05:15 means missing the ryokan breakfast or coming back for it at 07:15. Ask them for onigiri the night before and you buy back 90 minutes at Lake Tōya.

Sunset Jigokudani 地獄谷 16:42 ★

Low sun rakes sideways through the sulphur steam and backlights every vent. Five minutes on foot from your ryokan, so no driving after a long day.

🍁 Lake Tōya peaks mid-to-late October — in 2024 it ran Oct 15–25. You land inside the window, so this is your best foliage of the trip.
📷 Drive over Orofure on the way in, around 15:00. You shoot it at dawn tomorrow — seeing the deck, the railing and the parking in daylight first is worth the twenty minutes.

Orofure Pass
オロフレ峠 — ~930 m. Mt. Yōtei, Funka Bay, Lake Tōya and Kuttara all from one deck, with a real chance of a cloud sea below you. 100+ car spaces, toilets, ~25 min from the ryokan

Sunrise Orofure Pass オロフレ峠 05:53 ★★

The best light of the whole trip. Four landscapes at once from one railing, and the altitude gives you a genuine chance of standing above a sea of cloud.

Foliage here peaks early-to-mid October and drops about ten days after, so expect bare branches by the 24th. The panorama carries it regardless.

Sunset Mt. Moiwa 藻岩山 16:43 ★

Magic hour rolls straight into one of Japan's top-three night views without you moving a step. Go up around 15:30 and stay through blue hour. The summit runs 3–5 °C colder than the street, so bring a layer. ✨

⏰ Your fullest day. Pre-dawn Orofure, a 2 hr drive, the car return and then Moiwa at sunset — there is no slack in it. If anything slips, Moiwa is the part that survives being late.
